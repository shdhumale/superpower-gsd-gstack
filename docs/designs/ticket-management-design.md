## Problem Statement
Higher management has mandated a ticket tracking system with the primary driver being that critical and high-status tickets are getting lost in the noise, causing management to lack visibility into emergencies.

## Demand Evidence
The demand is currently a top-down mandate rather than organic ground-up demand. The pressing need is surfacing emergencies for leadership visibility.

## Status Quo
Issues are currently tracked poorly or not surfaced effectively, preventing management from seeing critical fires.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
Target user for the *demo*: The VP/Sponsor. 
Narrowest wedge: A lightweight `localStorage`-backed HTML dashboard that functions entirely within a single browser, featuring an obvious visual filter (a giant red "On Fire" or "Show Only Critical" button) to instantly prove the value of visual triage.

## Constraints
- Must be built extremely quickly to show momentum.
- No backend infrastructure for the initial prototype.
- Fully single-player for the demo.

## Premises
1. The fastest way to satisfy management is to ship a simplified dashboard prioritizing critical tickets over complex full-suite functionality.
2. The UI must explicitly color-code or default-filter the "Critical" status to prove its value immediately.
3. We are deferring complex search or multi-user backend sync to a V2; the goal today is a single-user prototype to prove the wedge.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: The Minimal Viable (Vanilla JS Filter Dashboard)
- Ships in hours; matches the current tech stack perfectly.
- Immediate visual payoff for the VP reading it.
- Single-player only for the demo (relies on `localStorage`).

### Approach B: The Ideal Architecture (Lightweight Native Backend)
- True multi-user experience.
- Requires deployment infrastructure and more effort.

### Approach C: The Creative/Lateral (Intranet Widget overlay)
- Intercepts management where they already work.
- Extreme "whoa" factor but distracts from the core app.

## Recommended Approach
Approach A: The Minimal Viable. It is the fastest, lowest-risk way to get a functional prototype in front of the sponsor to prove the wedge without getting bogged down in backend infrastructure.

## Open Questions
- How to manage the transition from this local prototype to a multi-player system after the sponsor approves the prototype.

## Success Criteria
- The VP/Sponsor can open the HTML file, add a critical ticket, and see it immediately highlighted and filtered over normal tickets.

## Distribution Plan
Share the application as a standalone folder or host the single `index.html` on an internal file share for the Vice President/Sponsor to play with locally.

## Dependencies
None.

## The Assignment
Before implementing, confirm exactly what the visual "Critical" state will look like (e.g., bright red card, flashing icon) so we can ensure the contrast stands out instantly to a non-technical manager.
